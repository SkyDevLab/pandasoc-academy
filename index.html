◉01
TRIAGE

Choose what matters

You have 18 alerts and time to investigate only five. Prioritise using asset criticality, account permissions, chronology and possible impact — not just the severity label.

Panda's checklist
  • What asset or identity is involved?
  • Does the activity deviate from its normal baseline?
  • What evidence and business context support the alert?

Record the priority and why. Follow the severity matrix used by your organisation.

⌕02
EVIDENCE

Validate, then scope

A security alert is a lead, not proof. Check the decisive log line, classify the alert, then see whether other events share accounts, hosts, IPs or a timeline.

Evidence that helps
  • Authentication results and source context
  • Endpoint process and network telemetry
  • Correlated events around the same time

Preserve relevant evidence according to policy before actions that may change it.

⌁03
INDICATORS

Spot the clues

IOCs are observable clues such as hashes, IPs and suspicious accounts. TTPs describe attacker behaviour such as persistence, discovery, lateral movement or exfiltration.

Indicator vs behaviour

An IP address or file hash may be an indicator. A sequence of unusual account changes and remote logons may indicate a tactic or technique.

Indicators can change quickly; behaviours and context help explain what might be happening.

⬡04
IMPACT

Assess and escalate

Assess functional impact, information impact and recoverability separately. Use the prescribed matrix, then escalate when scope, critical assets, reporting or time limits require it.

Three impact dimensions
  • Functional: which services or business processes are affected?
  • Information: what data may be exposed, changed or unavailable?
  • Recoverability: what effort and time are needed to restore?
▤05
COST

Count the impact

Separate direct, indirect and consequential costs. Show the arithmetic, use actual outage duration, and label assumptions and modelled estimates clearly.

Make estimates defensible

Keep known costs distinct from estimates. Document the source of each number, the period measured and assumptions used in the calculation.

Use your organisation's reporting methodology; this exercise does not prescribe a universal incident-cost formula.

DEFENDER TAKEAWAY

Don't rely on one clue.

Hashes and IPs can be valuable, but they may change. Correlate multiple signals and look for behaviours, while keeping false positives and environment context in mind.
